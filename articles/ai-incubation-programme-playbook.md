# AI Incubation Programme Playbook

A practical operating playbook for a university-linked AI and digital finance incubation programme.

This playbook translates AI market mapping into programme design: founder sourcing, selection, mentor matching, pilot readiness, investor readiness, research-to-venture translation, KPI reporting, demo day execution, and alumni tracking.

## 1. Programme Thesis

AI incubation should help founders move from promising ideas to credible evidence.

For a Singapore university-linked AI and digital finance programme, the operating thesis is:

> The best incubation programmes do not only provide events and advice. They help founders build evidence: customer evidence, product evidence, risk evidence, pilot evidence, and funding-readiness evidence.

The programme should focus on startups where AI creates value in a real workflow, especially in areas such as fintech, regtech, enterprise operations, AI governance, SME finance, research commercialisation, and responsible AI deployment.

### Core Operating Principles

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| Evidence before polish | Founders should validate customer pain before over-investing in pitch design. |
| Workflow before model | The programme should ask what user workflow improves, not only what model is used. |
| Risk readiness before pilots | AI-fintech startups need privacy, explainability, data, compliance, and governance thinking. |
| Partner fit before introductions | Corporate and institutional introductions should have a clear ask, pilot scope, and owner. |
| Reporting before demo day | Programme progress should be tracked weekly through evidence and milestone dashboards. |

## 2. Target Founder Profiles

The programme should support founders with different starting points, while using a consistent evidence-based operating model.

| Founder profile | Typical starting point | Main support need | Example output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student founder | Idea or early prototype | Customer discovery and MVP scoping | Problem statement and discovery evidence |
| Alumni startup | MVP or early traction | Go-to-market and funding readiness | Pitch pack and customer pipeline |
| Research team | Lab prototype or technical result | Research-to-venture translation | Commercialisation plan |
| Fintech operator | Domain insight and workflow pain | Productisation and pilot design | Pilot brief |
| Deep-tech founder | Technical asset or IP | Market validation and investor readiness | Technical-commercial roadmap |
| AI builder | Model, agent, or workflow tool | Buyer discovery and deployment readiness | Buyer persona and workflow map |
| Corporate spin-out team | Internal use case | Venture design and external market testing | Venture hypothesis and validation plan |

The cohort should avoid being too broad. A focused AI and digital finance programme works best when the majority of founders are solving workflow problems where Singapore has relevant buyers, mentors, research depth, or institutional partners.

## 3. Startup Sourcing Channels

Sourcing should be intentional. The goal is not only to collect applications, but to build a qualified venture pipeline.

| Channel | Who to source | Why it matters | Programme action |
|---|---|---|---|
| University entrepreneurship offices | Student and alumni founders | Early venture formation | Run founder clinics and info sessions |
| Research labs | Researchers and postgraduate teams | Deep technical assets | Host research-to-venture office hours |
| Fintech communities | Operators and startup teams | Sector-specific founder insight | Partner on founder scouting |
| Accelerators and incubators | MVP-stage startups | Existing startup pipeline | Create referral pathway |
| Corporate innovation teams | Internal problem owners and pilot partners | Real workflow pain | Source challenge statements |
| Venture studios | Operator-led startup concepts | Venture-building capability | Co-screen venture theses |
| Investor networks | High-potential early teams | Funding pathway visibility | Ask for founder referrals |
| Public-sector ecosystem programmes | Grant-ready or ecosystem-linked startups | Institutional support route | Coordinate application readiness |

### Sourcing Funnel

| Funnel stage | Target action | Evidence captured |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Publish problem themes and programme scope | Founder interest |
| Application | Collect founder, market, product, and evidence data | Application form |
| Screening | Review fit against rubric | Screening score |
| Interview | Test founder insight and coachability | Interview notes |
| Selection | Confirm cohort mix and support fit | Selection memo |
| Onboarding | Assign pathway, mentor, and first milestone | Founder support plan |

## 4. Selection Rubric

Selection should reward evidence, founder quality, and programme fit rather than pitch confidence alone.

Use a 100-point scoring model.

| Criterion | Weight | What to assess |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem clarity | 15 | Is the problem specific, painful, and tied to a real workflow? |
| Customer evidence | 15 | Has the team spoken to relevant users or buyers? |
| AI value proposition | 15 | Does AI materially improve the workflow or outcome? |
| Founder capability | 15 | Does the team have domain, technical, or execution credibility? |
| Product maturity | 10 | Is there an idea, prototype, MVP, or early traction? |
| Market and buyer clarity | 10 | Is there a plausible user, buyer, budget owner, or adoption path? |
| Risk and governance awareness | 10 | Has the team considered privacy, security, explainability, compliance, or model risk? |
| Programme fit | 10 | Can the programme realistically help through mentors, partners, pilots, or funding pathways? |

### Selection Bands

| Score | Decision | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 80-100 | Strong fit | Interview for cohort selection |
| 65-79 | Promising | Request more evidence or run a diagnostic session |
| 50-64 | Early but relevant | Offer pre-incubation support or founder clinic |
| Below 50 | Not ready | Provide feedback and reapplication pathway |

## 5. 10-Week Programme Structure

The programme should move founders from diagnosis to evidence, and from evidence to pilots or funding readiness.

| Week | Theme | Founder work | Programme deliverable |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Onboarding and diagnostic | Map founder stage, problem, customer, product, and risk gaps | Founder support plan |
| 2 | Customer discovery | Run interviews and validate problem intensity | Discovery tracker |
| 3 | Market and buyer mapping | Identify user, buyer, budget owner, and adoption path | Buyer persona and market map |
| 4 | MVP and workflow scoping | Define the smallest useful AI workflow | MVP scope and workflow map |
| 5 | Risk and governance review | Identify data, privacy, security, explainability, and compliance assumptions | Risk checklist |
| 6 | Mentor sprint | Match founders to domain, technical, product, go-to-market, or regulatory mentors | Mentor feedback memo |
| 7 | Pilot design | Define objective, partner type, metrics, data environment, timeline, and owner | Pilot brief |
| 8 | Funding and investor readiness | Build pitch, use-of-funds logic, milestones, and evidence pack | Investor or grant readiness pack |
| 9 | Demo day preparation | Refine narrative, demo, metrics, and partner ask | Demo day deck and one-page brief |
| 10 | Demo day and next pathway | Present to mentors, investors, corporates, and ecosystem partners | Post-programme action plan |

### Weekly Operating Rhythm

| Cadence | Purpose | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly founder check-in | Track progress and blockers | Updated milestone log |
| Mentor office hours | Solve specific founder bottlenecks | Mentor action notes |
| Programme review | Review cohort progress and risks | Internal dashboard |
| Partner matching review | Decide which teams are ready for introductions | Partner shortlist |
| Evidence review | Confirm what each team has learned | Evidence tracker |

## 6. Mentor and Partner Model

Mentor matching should be based on founder bottlenecks, not generic availability.

| Founder need | Best-fit mentor or partner | Example support |
|---|---|---|
| Customer discovery | Founder coach or industry operator | Interview design and problem framing |
| AI product architecture | Technical mentor | Model, data, evaluation, and workflow design |
| Fintech regulation | Regulatory or compliance mentor | Risk assumptions and sector constraints |
| Enterprise sales | B2B operator or corporate innovation lead | Buyer mapping and sales process |
| Pilot design | Corporate partner or programme lead | Pilot objective, metrics, and owner |
| Fundraising | Investor or venture mentor | Pitch narrative and investor readiness |
| Research commercialisation | Tech transfer or venture-building mentor | IP, founder role, and commercial pathway |

### Partner Roles

| Partner type | Contribution | Programme guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate partner | Use cases, buyer feedback, pilot environment | Do not introduce unprepared founders |
| Financial institution | Fintech workflow validation | Require risk and data boundary clarity |
| University lab | Research assets and technical depth | Translate research into customer need |
| Investor | Funding feedback and market signal | Separate advice from investment commitment |
| Public-sector ecosystem partner | Grant or ecosystem pathway visibility | Do not imply eligibility or guaranteed funding |
| Accelerator or incubator | Pipeline and founder support | Clarify ownership and handoff |

## 7. Pilot-Readiness Framework

Pilot readiness means the startup can run a focused, measurable test with a real partner without creating avoidable confusion or risk.

| Readiness area | Question | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Problem fit | Is the pilot solving a real partner problem? | Partner pain point and workflow map |
| Product maturity | Can the startup demonstrate the solution? | Demo, prototype, MVP, or sandbox |
| Data readiness | What data or environment is needed? | Data requirements and access assumptions |
| Risk awareness | What could block approval or adoption? | Privacy, security, explainability, and compliance checklist |
| Success metrics | How will the pilot be judged? | Quantitative and qualitative metrics |
| Ownership | Who owns the pilot on both sides? | Startup owner and partner owner |
| Timeline | Can the pilot be completed in a defined period? | Pilot plan and milestones |
| Post-pilot path | What happens after the pilot? | Decision options and follow-up plan |

### Pilot-Readiness Score

| Score | Readiness level | Recommended action |
|---:|---|---|
| 80-100 | Ready for partner introduction | Prepare introduction and pilot brief |
| 60-79 | Promising | Refine scope, metrics, and risk checklist |
| 40-59 | Not yet ready | Run more customer or workflow validation |
| Below 40 | Too early | Focus on discovery and product clarity |

## 8. Investor-Readiness Framework

Investor readiness is not only about a polished deck. It is about whether the startup can explain the market, evidence, product, team, and next milestone clearly.

| Area | Investor question | Founder evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Is the opportunity meaningful? | Market map, customer segment, buyer profile |
| Problem | Is the pain urgent? | Interview evidence and workflow pain |
| Product | Does the solution work? | Demo, MVP, technical roadmap |
| AI defensibility | Why is AI important here? | Data, workflow, evaluation, or technical edge |
| Traction | Is there evidence of demand? | Users, pilots, LOIs, revenue, or strong discovery signals |
| Business model | Can this become a business? | Pricing hypothesis and customer economics |
| Go-to-market | How will customers be reached? | Channel strategy and sales path |
| Team | Can this team execute? | Founder-market fit and capability |
| Use of funds | What will capital unlock? | Milestone-based funding plan |
| Risk | What could break the plan? | Risk register and mitigation plan |

### Funding-Readiness Outputs

- Investor deck
- One-page company brief
- Use-of-funds plan
- Milestone roadmap
- Customer evidence summary
- Pilot or traction summary
- Data and risk assumptions
- Target investor or grant pathway list

## 9. Research-to-Venture Translation Pathway

University-linked programmes need a clear path for turning research into venture propositions.

| Translation stage | Key question | Programme support | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research discovery | What technical asset exists? | Research review and inventor interview | Asset summary |
| Use-case framing | Who has the problem? | Market mapping and customer discovery | Use-case hypothesis |
| IP and ownership review | Can the asset be commercialised? | Tech transfer coordination | IP pathway note |
| Founder formation | Who will lead the venture? | Founder role and team design | Founder commitment plan |
| Prototype translation | Can non-research users test it? | MVP scoping and UX support | Testable prototype |
| Market validation | Does the market care? | Interviews and partner feedback | Validation evidence |
| Pilot pathway | Where can it be tested? | Partner matching and risk review | Pilot brief |
| Venture pathway | What is the next institutional route? | Grant, incubation, investor, or spin-off planning | Venture roadmap |

> Research-to-venture work is not a handoff from lab to market. It is an active translation process across technology, customer need, founder commitment, IP, and institutional support.

## 10. KPI Dashboard

The programme dashboard should track founder progress, partner activity, and venture outcomes.

| KPI category | Metric | Reporting use |
|---|---|---|
| Applications | Applications received, screened, interviewed, accepted | Pipeline visibility |
| Founder profile | Student, alumni, researcher, operator, spin-off, startup | Cohort composition |
| Customer discovery | Interviews completed, pain scores, validated use cases | Evidence quality |
| Product progress | Prototypes reviewed, MVP tests, demos completed | Product maturity |
| Mentor matching | Matches made, sessions completed, action items closed | Support effectiveness |
| Risk readiness | Risk checklists completed, data assumptions clarified | Pilot preparedness |
| Pilot readiness | Pilot briefs completed, partner matches, pilots scoped | External validation |
| Funding readiness | Pitch reviews, grant packs, investor meetings | Capital pathway readiness |
| Research translation | Research teams assessed, IP reviews, venture concepts | Lab-to-market progress |
| Programme outcomes | Incorporations, pilots launched, follow-on support, revenue leads | Venture outcomes |

### Cohort Health View

| Status | Meaning | Programme response |
|---|---|---|
| On track | Founder is meeting weekly evidence milestones | Continue current support |
| Needs support | Blocker is clear and solvable | Assign mentor or clinic |
| At risk | Founder has weak evidence or low momentum | Run intervention review |
| Ready for intro | Founder has clear ask and evidence | Prepare partner or investor introduction |
| Ready to graduate | Founder has next pathway | Confirm post-programme tracking plan |

## 11. Demo Day Format

Demo day should not be treated as a performance event only. It should be a conversion point for pilots, mentors, investors, partners, and next-step support.

### Suggested Format

| Segment | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Programme opening | 10 minutes | Explain cohort thesis and selection focus |
| Sector overview | 10 minutes | Frame AI and digital finance opportunity areas |
| Startup pitches | 4-5 minutes each | Present problem, product, evidence, ask |
| Expert Q&A | 3 minutes each | Test clarity and readiness |
| Partner matching session | 30 minutes | Connect startups to pilots, mentors, or buyers |
| Investor and grant pathway clinic | 30 minutes | Route teams to relevant capital pathways |
| Closing and follow-up | 10 minutes | Explain next-step process |

### Founder Pitch Structure

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Problem and target customer |
| 2 | Market and workflow pain |
| 3 | Product or AI solution |
| 4 | Evidence collected |
| 5 | Pilot, traction, or validation |
| 6 | Risk and deployment assumptions |
| 7 | Business model and go-to-market |
| 8 | Team |
| 9 | Ask: pilot, mentor, funding, partner, or customer |

### Demo Day Follow-Up

- Send partner-specific startup briefs.
- Record investor and partner interest.
- Assign follow-up owners.
- Track meetings booked within two weeks.
- Update alumni dashboard with next milestones.

## 12. Post-Programme Alumni Tracking

Post-programme tracking is where incubation quality becomes visible.

Many outcomes happen after the formal cohort ends, so the programme should continue measuring venture progress.

| Tracking area | 30 days | 90 days | 6 months |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder progress | Next milestone completed | Product or pilot progress | Venture status update |
| Customer evidence | New interviews or users | Customer pipeline | Revenue or paid pilot signals |
| Pilot activity | Meetings booked | Pilot scoped or launched | Pilot completed or expanded |
| Funding pathway | Grant or investor prep | Applications or meetings | Funding, follow-on support, or revised plan |
| Mentor engagement | Follow-up session | Mentor outcome | Ongoing advisory relationship |
| Research translation | IP or commercialisation review | Venture formation step | Spin-off or licensing pathway |
| Ecosystem value | Partner feedback | New introductions | Repeat partner collaboration |

### Alumni Operating Rhythm

| Cadence | Action |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Founder progress survey |
| Quarterly | Alumni review and KPI update |
| Biannually | Partner and investor follow-up |
| Annually | Outcomes report and programme improvement review |

The alumni system should answer one practical question:

> Which founders are still progressing, what evidence have they gained, and what support would unlock the next step?

---

## Closing Thesis

A strong AI and digital finance incubation programme is an operating system for venture progress.

It should source the right founders, screen for evidence, assign targeted support, coordinate mentors and partners, prepare pilots, build investor readiness, translate research into venture pathways, and track outcomes beyond demo day.

The goal is not to make every startup look polished. The goal is to help each founder make better decisions, gather stronger evidence, and move toward a credible next milestone.
